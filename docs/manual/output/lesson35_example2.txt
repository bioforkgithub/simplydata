Groups made alike in age, educ, race, married, nodegree, re74 and re75 (matching), then compared: "re78" in "1" against "0" (614 rows: 185 and 429).
Propensity score: each row's chance of being in "1" given age, educ, race, married, nodegree, re74 and re75, from logistic regression (R glm(family = binomial)).
Overlap: the scores run from 0.00564 to 0.966 in "1" and from 0 to 0.862 in "0"; 33 rows of "1" and 70 of "0" lie outside the other group's range (no row like them in the other group: their effect is guessed from the model).

Note: 51 rows of "1" had no match within 0.200 standard deviations and were left out, so the effect is for the matched rows of that group only.
Effect for those in "1" (the effect among them, ATT): the average of "re78" is 1010 higher in "1" than in "0" (95 % CI 10.7 to 2008), once the groups are made alike.
If the groups, once made alike, truly did not differ, a difference this big or bigger would turn up in about 5 of 100 studies like this one (p = 0.048).
This is the effect of "treat" only if nothing that was not measured affects both "treat" and "re78" (the columns of allowing_for= are all that matter), every row could have been in either group (the scores overlap), and the propensity model is right. Balancing cannot make up for a column that was not measured.
Method: 1:1 nearest-neighbour matching on the propensity score without replacement (R MatchIt::matchit(method = "nearest", distance = "glm", caliper 0.200 standard deviations of the logit of the score (link = "linear.logit"))), the effect from lm() on the matched rows with standard errors clustered on the pairs (sandwich::vcovCL, t test), as the MatchIt vignette does; balance from R cobalt::bal.tab().
