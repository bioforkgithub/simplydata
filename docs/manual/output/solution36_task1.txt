Comparing the average "procalcitonin" in ward A and ward B the Bayesian way (100 and 100 rows).
The data are 2.98 times as likely if they do not differ as if the averages of ward A and ward B differ (Bayes factor for a difference: 0.335, with the JZS prior: a Cauchy prior on the effect size (the difference in standard deviations) with width 0.707 ("medium", BayesFactor's default; Rouder et al. 2009)).
Given the data and the prior, the difference (ward A minus ward B) is probably between -0.197 and 0.0425 (95 % credible interval: given the data and the prior, a 95 % chance that the value lies in it); its middle value (the posterior median) is -0.0746. The effect size (the difference in standard deviations) is probably between -0.445 and 0.0963.
Method: R BayesFactor::ttestBF(x, y) (rscale = "medium"); the interval from 10,000 draws of the posterior (BayesFactor::posterior()), with_seed=1: the same seed gives the same draws again.
A Bayes factor is not the chance that a hypothesis is true: it says how much the data shift the odds between the two (Wagenmakers et al. 2018). It depends on the prior width; prior_width="wide" shows how much. There is no p-value in a Bayesian answer.
