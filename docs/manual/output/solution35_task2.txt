Groups made alike in age, educ, race, married, nodegree, re74 and re75 (weighting), then compared: "re78" in "1" against "0" (614 rows: 185 and 429).
Propensity score: each row's chance of being in "1" given age, educ, race, married, nodegree, re74 and re75, from logistic regression (R glm(family = binomial)).
Overlap: the scores run from 0.00564 to 0.966 in "1" and from 0 to 0.862 in "0"; 33 rows of "1" and 70 of "0" lie outside the other group's range (no row like them in the other group: their effect is guessed from the model).

Effect for those in "1" (the effect among them, ATT): the average of "re78" is 1321 higher in "1" than in "0" (95 % CI 555 to 2088), once the groups are made alike.
If the groups, once made alike, truly did not differ, a difference this big or bigger would turn up in fewer than 1 in 1000 studies like this one (p = 0.0007).
This is the effect of "treat" only if nothing that was not measured affects both "treat" and "re78" (the columns of allowing_for= are all that matter), every row could have been in either group (the scores overlap), and the propensity model is right. Balancing cannot make up for a column that was not measured.
Method: inverse probability weighting (R WeightIt::weightit(method = "glm", estimand = "ATT")), the effect from WeightIt::glm_weightit(family = gaussian), as lm_weightit(), with standard errors that allow for the estimated weights (M-estimation, z test); balance from R cobalt::bal.tab().
