Combining 13 studies (random effects, REML, Hartung-Knapp): risk ratio of "tb_vaccinated" out of "vaccinated" against "tb_unvaccinated" out of "unvaccinated".
Combined risk ratio 0.489 (95 % CI 0.330 to 0.726, Hartung-Knapp; 13 studies).
If there were truly no effect on average (a risk ratio of 1), a combined effect this far from 1 or further would turn up in about 2 of 1000 sets of studies like these (p = 0.002).
In a new setting like these studies, the true risk ratio would lie between 0.136 and 1.76 (95 % prediction interval, t with 12 degrees of freedom, as metafor uses with Hartung-Knapp; Higgins et al. 2009 use 11).
The true risk ratios typically differ from the average by a factor of about 1.75 up or down (tau = 0.560 on the log scale; tau-squared = 0.313, 95 % CI 0.120 to 1.11).
I-squared = 92 % (95 % CI 82 to 98 %): the share of the spread in the study results that is beyond chance; it says how much of the spread is real, not how big it is.
If every study truly measured the same effect, differences between the studies this big or bigger would turn up in fewer than 1 in 1000 sets of studies like these (Q = 152, 12 degrees of freedom, p < 0.0001).
Small-study effects (Peters' test, for risk and odds ratios from counts): If small and large studies truly showed the same effect, a link between study size and effect this strong or stronger would turn up in about 23 of 100 sets of studies like these (t = -1.27, 11 degrees of freedom, p = 0.230). This does not show that there are no small-study effects. Small-study effects are not proof of publication bias: they can also come from real differences between small and large studies, or from chance, and this test does not say which.
Trim and fill (a what-if, not a correction): 1 study might be missing on the right; with it the combined risk ratio would be 0.518 (95 % CI 0.365 to 0.736, normal interval, as metafor trimfill() refits). With this much difference between studies (I-squared above 50 %), trim and fill is a rough guess.
Method: R metafor::escalc(measure = "RR", ai, n1i, ci, n2i, drop00 = TRUE), then metafor::rma(yi, vi, method = "REML", test = "adhoc", control = list(tol = 1e-10, threshold = 1e-10, maxiter = 1000)); predict() for the combined effect and the prediction interval, confint() for the intervals of tau-squared and I-squared; Hartung-Knapp (for the interval and the p-value) with the ad hoc rule, so it is never narrower than the normal interval; small-study effects by metafor::regtest(model = "lm", predictor = "ninv") with Peters' weights and metafor::trimfill().
simplydata chooses: random effects with REML, Hartung-Knapp intervals for random effects (never narrower than the normal interval), the prediction interval from 3 studies, and the notes on small-study effects from 10 studies (Peters' test for risk and odds ratios from counts, Egger's test for the rest); test_method= and interval_by= change them.
