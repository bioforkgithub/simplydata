Combining 37 studies (random effects, REML, Hartung-Knapp): "odds ratio" (ratios, combined on the log scale and shown back as ratios; estimates_are_ratios=False says they are not).
Combined odds ratio 1.24 (95 % CI 1.13 to 1.38, Hartung-Knapp; 37 studies).
If there were truly no effect on average (an odds ratio of 1), a combined effect this far from 1 or further would turn up in fewer than 1 in 1000 sets of studies like these (p < 0.0001).
In a new setting like these studies, the true odds ratio would lie between 0.904 and 1.71 (95 % prediction interval, t with 36 degrees of freedom, as metafor uses with Hartung-Knapp; Higgins et al. 2009 use 35).
The true odds ratios typically differ from the average by a factor of about 1.16 up or down (tau = 0.150 on the log scale; tau-squared = 0.0224, 95 % CI 0 to 0.0664).
I-squared = 30 % (95 % CI 0 to 55 %): the share of the spread in the study results that is beyond chance; it says how much of the spread is real, not how big it is.
If every study truly measured the same effect, differences between the studies this big or bigger would turn up in about 10 of 100 sets of studies like these (Q = 47.5, 36 degrees of freedom, p = 0.095). This does not show that the studies measured the same effect.
Small-study effects (Egger's regression test): If small and large studies truly showed the same effect, a link between study size and effect this strong or stronger would turn up in about 2 of 100 sets of studies like these (t = 2.38, 35 degrees of freedom, p = 0.023). Small-study effects are not proof of publication bias: they can also come from real differences between small and large studies, or from chance, and this test does not say which.
Trim and fill (a what-if, not a correction): 7 studies might be missing on the left; with them the combined odds ratio would be 1.19 (95 % CI 1.08 to 1.31, normal interval, as metafor trimfill() refits).
Method: R metafor::conv.wald(out, ci.lb, ci.ub, level = 95, transf = log), then metafor::rma(yi, vi, method = "REML", test = "adhoc"); predict() for the combined effect and the prediction interval, confint() for the intervals of tau-squared and I-squared; Hartung-Knapp (for the interval and the p-value) with the ad hoc rule, so it is never narrower than the normal interval; small-study effects by metafor::regtest(model = "lm", predictor = "sei") and metafor::trimfill().
simplydata chooses: random effects with REML, Hartung-Knapp intervals for random effects (never narrower than the normal interval), the prediction interval from 3 studies, and the notes on small-study effects from 10 studies (Peters' test for risk and odds ratios from counts, Egger's test for the rest); test_method= and interval_by= change them.
