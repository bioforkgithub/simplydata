Checking the model of "marks" (180 rows) in four pictures, as R plot(lm(marks ~ hours + school)):
  1. residuals against fitted values: an even band around 0, with no curve, means the form fits;
  2. normal Q-Q: dots on the line mean the residuals follow a bell curve;
  3. scale-location: a flat line means the spread is the same everywhere;
  4. residuals against leverage: rows beyond the dashed lines (Cook's distance 0.5 and 1) pull the model strongly.
Numbers from statsmodels (OLSInfluence or GLMInfluence); the smooth lines are LOWESS, as R panel.smooth. Pictures help judge a model better than tests of the residuals.
How strongly each column is linked with the others (variance inflation factor, R car::vif() itself): "hours" 1.08 and "school" 1.08 (generalised, 11 df; 1.00 on the scale of one column). Above 5 a column's effect is hard to tell apart from the others'.
Comparing two models of "marks" on the same 180 rows:
  using= marks ~ hours + school: AIC = 1224, BIC = 1269 (14 values estimated)
  against= marks ~ hours + school + breakfast: AIC = 1219, BIC = 1266 (15 values estimated)
Lower AIC: the against= model (by 5.57).
Does "breakfast" help? F test (F = 7.13, df = 1 and 166): unlikely to be chance (p = 0.008).
Method: both models fitted as lm(marks ~ hours + school + breakfast) and the smaller one, then an F test, as R anova(m0, m1) (statsmodels compare_f_test); AIC and BIC as R AIC() and BIC().
