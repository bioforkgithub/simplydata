Explaining "marks" from "hours" and "breakfast" the Bayesian way (180 rows). Each effect below holds the other columns fixed:
  intercept: 32.3 (28.9 to 35.7), the expected "marks" when every number column is 0 and every group column is at its baseline.
  hours: each 1 more "hours" goes with 2.82 more "marks" (2.31 to 3.35).
  breakfast (yes) against the baseline group: "marks" is 3.21 higher (0.507 to 5.88).
  spread around the model (sigma): 8.67 (7.81 to 9.64) in "marks".
Each number: the posterior median with its 95 % credible interval: given the data and the prior, a 95 % chance that the value lies in it. There are no p-values in a Bayesian model.

Convergence: R-hat at most 1.00 (rank-normalised; the guide is 1.01 or below), at least 4868 effective draws in the middle and 2199 in the tails (the guide: 400 or more), 0 divergent transitions; over all 4 parameters and 4,000 draws, the posterior package's rhat(), ess_bulk() and ess_tail() (Vehtari et al. 2021).
Method: R rstanarm::stan_glm(marks ~ hours + breakfast, family = gaussian(), chains = 4, iter = 2000, seed = with_seed): 4 chains of 1000 draws after 1000 warm-up, the posterior median and the central 95 % interval (posterior_interval()); with_seed=1 gives the same draws again on the same computer. The prior scales follow the spread of the data, so they change when the data change.
