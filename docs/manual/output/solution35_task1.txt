Effect for those in "1" (the effect among them, ATT): the average of "re78" is 894 higher in "1" than in "0" (95 % CI -492 to 2281), once the groups are made alike.
If the groups, once made alike, truly did not differ, a difference this big or bigger would turn up in about 21 of 100 studies like this one (p = 0.206). This does not show that there is no difference.
This is the effect of "treat" only if nothing that was not measured affects both "treat" and "re78" (the columns of allowing_for= are all that matter), every row could have been in either group (the scores overlap), and the propensity model is right. Balancing cannot make up for a column that was not measured.
Method: 1:1 nearest-neighbour matching on the propensity score without replacement (R MatchIt::matchit(method = "nearest", distance = "glm")), the effect from lm() on the matched rows with standard errors clustered on the pairs (sandwich::vcovCL, t test), as the MatchIt vignette does; balance from R cobalt::bal.tab().
