Small-study effects (Peters' test, for risk and odds ratios from counts): If small and large studies truly showed the same effect, a link between study size and effect this strong or stronger would turn up in about 42 of 100 sets of studies like these (t = -0.847, 11 degrees of freedom, p = 0.415). This does not show that there are no small-study effects. Small-study effects are not proof of publication bias: they can also come from real differences between small and large studies, or from chance, and this test does not say which.
Trim and fill (a what-if, not a correction): 3 studies might be missing on the right; with them the combined risk ratio would be 0.548 (95 % CI 0.418 to 0.718, normal interval, as metafor trimfill() refits). With this much difference between studies (I-squared above 50 %), trim and fill is a rough guess.
Method: R metafor::escalc(measure = "RR", ai, n1i, ci, n2i, drop00 = TRUE), then metafor::rma(yi, vi, method = "REML", test = "adhoc", control = list(tol = 1e-10, threshold = 1e-10, maxiter = 1000)); predict() for the combined effect and the prediction interval, confint() for the intervals of tau-squared and I-squared; each subgroup its own rma(), and the test of their difference rma(estimates, sei, mods = ~ group, method = "EE"); Hartung-Knapp (for the interval and the p-value) with the ad hoc rule, so it is never narrower than the normal interval; small-study effects by metafor::regtest(model = "lm", predictor = "ninv") with Peters' weights and metafor::trimfill().
